# 3D in Bronto

Bronto draws 3D the way it draws 2D: in software, into the same framebuffer, with no GPU API
and nothing outside the C standard library. The 3D renderer is a depth-buffered triangle
rasterizer with perspective-correct textures, per-vertex lighting and clipping, so 3D
games run everywhere the engine does, the web included. It suits low resolutions (a
320 x 180 or 640 x 360 game) and scenes of a few thousand triangles: think early console
3D, not a modern GPU.

A scene can mix both. 3D is drawn first, through the scene's camera, and 2D (Shapes,
Sprites, Labels...) on top of it, which makes 2D the natural place for a HUD.

## Coordinates

- **Right-handed, y up.** x points right, y up, and z towards the viewer, so a camera with
  no rotation looks down **-z**. "Forward" is -z everywhere in the engine.
- **Units** are whatever you decide; physics assumes metres (gravity is 9.81 down).
  The built-in shapes are 1 unit across.
- **Rotation** is three angles in degrees: `x` pitch (positive tips the nose up), `y` yaw
  (positive turns left, seen from above), `z` roll. They are applied yaw, then pitch, then
  roll, so a camera's rotation reads naturally: `rotation = 0 90 0` faces -x.
- Triangles are wound counter-clockwise when seen from the front.

## Components

### Transform3D

What makes an entity 3D: `position`, `rotation` (degrees) and `scale` (three numbers).
Parents work as in 2D (a Parent component, or dragging in the editor's hierarchy): a child's
transform is relative to its parent's.

### Mesh

Something to see.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `shape` | `cube`, `sphere`, `plane` (flat, facing up), `cylinder`, `cone`, `capsule` (1 across, 2 tall), or `model` |
| `file` | with `model`: a Wavefront `.obj` file, or a glTF `.gltf` / `.glb` (with its colours, textures, skeleton and animations) |
| `texture` | an image (`.png`, `.bmp` or `.tga`) wrapped over it, or empty for a plain colour |
| `color` | the colour; with a texture, it tints it. Alpha below 255 makes it see-through |
| `lit` | lit by the scene's lights (off: always full brightness, for skies and glowing things) |
| `cull` | hide the back faces (turn off for flat things seen from both sides) |
| `visible` | drawn at all |
| `uv_scale` | how many times the texture repeats across it (`4 4` tiles a floor) |
| `shadow` | a soft round shadow on whatever is below it (off by default; for scenes without a Light casting real shadows) |
| `cast_shadow` | blocks the light of a Light with `shadows` on (on by default) |

`.obj` files: vertices (`v`), texture coordinates (`vt`), normals (`vn`) and faces (`f`)
with any number of corners. Without normals, each face gets its own (flat shading).
Materials, groups and smoothing lines are ignored: give the Mesh a `texture` and `color`
instead. Most modelling programs can export `.obj`.

### Camera3D

What the game shows. (The 2D **Camera** component is a different thing: it moves the 2D
world, and in a 3D scene it only affects the 2D layers drawn on top.) The active camera with the highest `priority` is used.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `projection` | `perspective` or `orthographic` |
| `fov` | perspective: the view's height, in degrees (60 by default) |
| `size` | orthographic: half the view's height, in units |
| `near_clip`, `far_clip` | nothing nearer or further than these is drawn |
| `active`, `priority` | which camera is used |
| `clear`, `clear_color` | fill the screen with this colour first (the sky) |
| `fog`, `fog_color` | fade distant things into this colour (usually the sky's) |
| `fog_start`, `fog_end` | where the fog begins, and where it hides things completely (units from the camera) |

### Light

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `kind` | `directional` (the sun: shines along the entity's forward, -z), `point` (a bulb: from where the entity is, fading out at `range`), or `ambient` (everywhere, from no direction) |
| `color`, `intensity` | how bright, in what colour |
| `range` | point lights: how far they reach |
| `shadows` | directional lights: real shadows, cast by every Mesh with `cast_shadow` onto everything lit |
| `shadow_range` | ... over this many units around where the camera looks (smaller: sharper) |

Up to 8 lights count at once. A scene without lights gets a default sun, so things are
visible straight away.

### Animated models

A glTF model (`.glb`, or `.gltf` with its `.bin` and images beside it), exported from
Blender or most other 3D tools, can carry a skeleton and animations. Give its entity a
Mesh (`shape` model, `file` the model) and an **Animator**:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `clip` | the animation playing, by its name in the file (`walk`, `idle`...) |
| `speed` | 1 as made, 2 twice as fast, negative backwards |
| `loop` | round and round; else once, then held at its end (`playing` turns false) |
| `playing` | moving on |
| `blend` | changing `clip` crossfades over this many seconds |

From a script, `play_animation(self, "walk")` does the same (see the scripting reference).
The editor plays the clip while you edit, too. Skinning (up to four joints a vertex) is
done on the CPU; physics and picking use the model at rest. glTF models face +z; Bronto's
forward is -z, so turn one 180 degrees (or export it facing -z). `tools/gltf/gltf_writer.py`
makes simple boxy ones from Python, as Explorer's figure is made.

### RigidBody3D, Collider3D, PhysicsWorld3D

3D physics, as in 2D:

- **RigidBody3D**: `type` dynamic (gravity and collisions move it), static or kinematic
  (moves only by its own velocity, and pushes dynamic bodies). `mass`, `gravity_scale`,
  `velocity` (units / second), `angular_velocity` (degrees / second about x, y and z),
  `linear_damping`, `angular_damping`, and `lock_rotation` (keeps a character upright).
- **Collider3D**: a `box` (`size`: width, height, depth), a `sphere` (`radius`), a
  `capsule` (`radius`, and `size.y` for its whole height, along the entity's y) or a
  `mesh` (the triangles of the entity's Mesh, for terrain and level geometry; only for
  static and kinematic things), moved by `offset`, turned and scaled with the entity. `friction`, `bounce`, `trigger` (reports
  overlaps, doesn't push), `layer` and `mask` (which layers it meets). A collider without a
  RigidBody3D is static scenery.
- **PhysicsWorld3D** (optional, on any one entity): `gravity` (default `0 -9.81 0`) and
  solver `iterations`.

Boxes stack, spheres roll, things come to rest. Contacts are found with separating axes and
solved with sequential impulses, with friction, bounce and warm starting, at the fixed 60
steps a second. Scripts hear about contacts through `on_collision`, `on_collision_end`,
`on_trigger` and `on_trigger_exit`, with a vec3 normal.

### Characters

A player who walks rather than rolls: give it a capsule Collider3D and move it from its
script with `move_and_slide(self, velocity)`. It moves by `velocity * dt`, slides along
walls, walks up slopes and stays on the ground, and returns the velocity with the parts
that went into things taken out, to keep for the next frame. `is_on_floor(e)`,
`is_on_wall(e)` and `is_on_ceiling(e)` say what the last move met, for jumping and landing:

```
vel.y -= 20 * dt
if is_on_floor(self) and key_pressed("space") { vel.y = 7.5 }
vel = move_and_slide(self, vel)
```

A kinematic RigidBody3D on it (with no velocity: the physics then leaves it alone) lets it
set off triggers. The editor's **New 3D > Character** makes one: a capsule mesh and
collider, a kinematic body and a shadow. From C: `br_physics3d_move(world, e, motion,
&velocity, &info)`.

### Particles

The Particles component (see [the scripting reference](scripting.md#particles)) works in 3D
too, on an entity with a Transform3D: sizes, speeds and gravity are in units, and they
leave along the entity's up (+y) within a cone of `spread` degrees (180: every way;
a negative `speed` sends them down, for rain). They are drawn after the meshes as flat dots
facing the camera, hidden behind what's in front of them and faded into the fog.
**New 3D > Particles** in the editor adds a fire to start from.

## Scripts

A script for a 3D entity starts with `@3d`; then `self.position`, `self.rotation`,
`self.velocity`... are `vec3`s, and `self.z`, `self.forward`, `self.right` and `self.up`
exist. There are 3D versions of the physics functions (`apply_force`, `apply_impulse`,
`apply_torque`, `raycast`, `entity_at`, `overlap_sphere`), `look_at`, and
`world_to_screen` / `screen_ray` for HUDs and mouse picking. See
[the scripting reference](scripting.md#3d).

## The editor

A scene with 3D entities opens in 3D; the toolbar's **3D** button switches the view by hand.

- **Right-drag** orbits around the focus point, **middle-drag** (or Shift + right-drag) pans,
  the **wheel** zooms. **F** frames the selection.
- **Click** selects: meshes by their surface; cameras, lights and empty entities by their
  markers (a small frustum, a sun, a set of axes).
- The gizmo: **W** move (drag an arrow along that axis, or the centre square across the
  ground), **E** rotate (drag a ring), **R** scale (drag a box along the entity's own axis,
  or the centre to scale evenly). **Snap** rounds to half units, 15 degrees and tenths.
- **C** (or the Camera button) looks through the scene's Camera3D, as the game will.
- **Right-click** in the view: **New 3D** adds a cube, sphere, plane, cylinder, cone, capsule,
  character, camera, light or empty where you clicked. The hierarchy's right-click menu has the same.
- Drag an `.obj` from the files into the view to place it; drag an image onto a mesh to
  texture it; drag a prefab scene to place a copy on the ground. The file preview shows
  models turning.
- **Colliders** shows Collider3D boxes, spheres, capsules and meshes; **Play** runs the scene, physics and all.

## From C

The 3D layer is usable without the ECS too:

```c
#include "bronto/bronto.h"

br_mesh cube;
br_r3d r3;
br_camera3d cam = br_camera3d_make(br_v3(3, 2, 5), br_v3(0, 0, 0));   /* from, looking at */
br_material red = br_material_make(br_rgb(220, 60, 50));

br_mesh_cube(&cube);
br_r3d_init(&r3);
/* each frame, after clearing: */
br_r3d_begin(&r3, &engine->renderer, &cam, br_recti_make(0, 0, w, h));
br_r3d_draw_mesh(&r3, &cube, br_m4_trs(br_v3(0, 0.5f, 0), br_quat_from_euler(br_v3(0, angle, 0)), br_v3(1, 1, 1)), &red);
br_r3d_line(&r3, br_v3(0, 0, 0), br_v3(2, 0, 0), BR_RED, true);
```

- `bmath.h`: `br_vec3`, `br_quat` (`br_quat_from_euler`, `br_quat_rotate`, `br_quat_slerp`,
  `br_quat_look`), `br_mat4` (`br_m4_trs`, `br_m4_decompose`).
- `render3d.h`: meshes (`br_mesh_cube/sphere/plane/cylinder/cone/capsule`, `br_mesh_load_obj`,
  `br_mesh_raycast`), cameras, lights, materials, and the `br_r3d` pass (`begin`,
  `add_light`, `draw_mesh`, `line`, `project`, `ray`).
- `components3d.h`: the components above, world transforms (`br_world_matrix3d`,
  `br_set_world_position3d`, `br_forward3d`, `br_look_at3d`), `br_draw3d` (a world through
  any camera), `br_pick3d` (the mesh under a ray).
- `physics3d.h`: `br_physics3d_step`, forces, `br_physics3d_raycast`,
  `br_physics3d_overlap_sphere`, `br_physics3d_move` / `br_physics3d_last_move` (characters),
  `br_physics3d_debug_draw`.

The engine registers all of these with the other built-in components; `br_world_has_3d()`
tells whether a scene uses them.

## The examples

`examples/marble` is a small 3D game made only of scenes and scripts: roll a marble around
a course of ramps and blocks, collect the gems, and don't fall off. Open its folder in the
editor to see how it is put together, or play it with `make marble`.

`examples/explorer` walks instead: an animated figure (a glTF model with a skeleton: idle,
walk, jump and cheer) on rolling hills (a model, which is
also its mesh collider) with trees, rocks and stepping stones, fog in the distance and
real shadows from the sun, and glowing crystals that burst into light when found. Find the eight. `make explorer` plays it.
